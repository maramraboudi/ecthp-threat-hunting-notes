# Study plan and scope

## A four-week path

| Week | Focus | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | SOC foundations, CTI concepts, intelligence types, and lifecycle | One-page PIR-to-collection plan. |
| 2 | Data quality, OSINT, STIX/TAXII/TLP, analytic models, and TIPs | Source evaluation and short analytic assessment. |
| 3 | Hunting fundamentals, hunt strategies, and CTI-to-hypothesis workflow | A scoped hunt hypothesis with a telemetry matrix. |
| 4 | Network and endpoint hunting, reporting, and capstone | Hunt record, evidence summary, and detection follow-up. |

Adjust the pace to your background. Spend more time on logging, network fundamentals, Windows event data, and query-language basics if those are new.

## A repeatable study loop

1. Read a module and write its key distinction in your own words.
2. Sketch the workflow or model from memory.
3. Apply it to a small, synthetic scenario.
4. Identify assumptions, missing data, and alternative explanations.
5. Explain the result to both a technical peer and a non-technical stakeholder.
6. Revisit the notes after a delay and answer the review prompts without looking.

## Certification preparation

These notes support general CTI and threat-hunting learning relevant to eCTHP study. They are not an official blueprint and do not claim complete coverage of the current eCTHP exam. Check INE's current certification page, course catalog, policies, and candidate guidance. Practice fundamentals and reasoning rather than memorizing answer keys.

## Scope and safe practice

Exercises must use your own isolated lab, synthetic events, public training data, or systems explicitly authorized for testing. Define scope, allowed data, time range, and stop conditions. Never turn examples into probes of public hosts. Avoid collection or publication of personal data, credentials, session tokens, or sensitive indicators. Preserve evidence only as permitted by the relevant policy and law.

When a finding suggests an active incident, follow the organization's incident-response escalation process rather than continuing an uncoordinated hunt.
